The Process

The Journey of Blood

From your arm to saving a life — click through each stage to understand exactly what happens.

Registration & Health Check
Mini-Physical Exam
  • Temperature: Must be below 99.5°F / 37.5°C
  • Blood Pressure: Systolic 90–180 mm Hg, diastolic under 100 mm Hg
  • Pulse: Regular rhythm, 50–100 BPM
  • Hemoglobin: At least 12.5 g/dL (women) / 13.0 g/dL (men)
Health History Questionnaire
  • Recent travel to malaria-risk or other flagged regions
  • Current medications and dosages
  • Previous surgeries, transfusions, or medical procedures
  • Exposure risk factors assessed confidentially
The Donation
Whole Blood
  • Most common donation type
  • Takes 8–10 minutes to collect
  • ~500 mL (one pint) collected
  • Separated into components later
Apheresis
  • Machine separates specific components
  • Takes 1–2 hours total
  • Plasma or platelets collected
  • Rest of blood returned to donor
Step-by-Step: What Happens
  • A sterile, single-use needle is inserted into the antecubital vein (inner elbow area)
  • Blood flows through tubing into a sterile collection bag with anticoagulant to prevent clotting
  • The bag is on a rocker that gently mixes blood as it fills — takes under 10 minutes
  • Needle removed, pressure applied. You're done. Go eat the cookies.
Laboratory Processing
Centrifugation: Blood Gets Split
  • Red Blood Cells (~45%): Carry oxygen. Used for trauma, surgery, anemia
  • Plasma (~55%): Liquid part. Contains clotting factors and antibodies
  • Platelets (<1%): Tiny cell fragments critical for clotting. Vital for cancer patients
Disease Testing
  • HIV-1 and HIV-2
  • Hepatitis B and C
  • Syphilis, Zika, West Nile
  • Chagas disease (where endemic)
Blood Typing
  • ABO group confirmed
  • Rh factor determined
  • Unexpected antibody screen
  • Extended antigen typing if needed

Eligibility Explorer

Are You Eligible?

Answer 5 questions to learn about real eligibility criteria. Educational only — not a medical screening.

Question 1 / 5
You now know the basics.

General guidelines — always confirmed by trained staff at your donation center using up-to-date regulatory requirements.

Basic Requirements
Age 16–17 (with parental consent in some regions) or 18+, weight ≥110 lbs / 50 kg, good general health on donation day
Donation Frequency
Whole blood: every 56 days. Platelets: every 7 days (max 24/year). Plasma: every 28 days
Common Deferrals
Recent tattoos / piercings, certain medications, travel to malaria-risk areas, low hemoglobin, illness on donation day
Myth-Busting

Myth vs. Fact

Click any card to flip it and get the truth. Some of these are genuinely wild.

"I'll get HIV from donating blood"
Tap to reveal →
Completely False
Every needle is sterile, single-use, and disposed of immediately. It's physically impossible to contract anything from donating. All equipment touches only you.
"I don't have enough blood to spare"
Tap to reveal →
False — Your Body's Got You
Adults carry ~10 pints of blood. You donate 1. Plasma is restored within 24–48 hours. Red cells take 4–6 weeks — which is exactly why there's a 56-day wait.
"Vegetarians can't donate"
Tap to reveal →
False — Diet Doesn't Disqualify
What matters is hemoglobin level, not protein source. Vegetarians with adequate iron intake donate all the time. Lentils, spinach, tofu — your blood doesn't care.
"Diabetics can never donate"
Tap to reveal →
Mostly False
Well-controlled Type 2 diabetics and many Type 1 donors can donate if their condition is stable and no vascular complications exist. Insulin type matters — check locally.
"Tattoos permanently disqualify you"
Tap to reveal →
False — Just a Waiting Period
Done at a regulated parlor? Many regions allow immediate donation. Unregulated = 3–12 month deferral. In the US, FDA updated rules in 2023 to reduce the wait significantly.
"It hurts a lot"
Tap to reveal →
False — It's a 2-Second Pinch
The needle insertion is a quick sting, like a vaccine. Then nothing. Most donors scroll their phone the entire time. The anxiety is genuinely worse than the needle.
